# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary audience — Venezuelans abroad** (US, Chile, Colombia, Spain, etc.) who have an elderly parent, grandparent, or dependent family member still in Venezuela. They send remittances, they worry, and they carry the guilt of the distance. They are not looking for "elderly care" in the abstract — they are looking for someone to go where they cannot go. The page talks to them directly.

**Secondary audience — the family in Venezuela** who will receive the visits. They are not the ones signing up, but they are present in every sentence; the Compa serves them, and the page must feel like something they would trust welcoming into their home.

The page addresses both simultaneously. Copy that makes the person abroad feel understood must also feel safe and dignified to the person who will receive the visits.

## Product Purpose

Compaz connects Venezuelan diaspora families with verified local "Compas" who visit, accompany, and care for elderly or dependent relatives left in Venezuela. After each visit, the Compa sends a WhatsApp report with photos and notes so the person abroad knows how their family is — not just imagines it.

Success looks like: a person abroad discovers Compaz through social media or word of mouth, immediately recognizes the problem it solves, and joins the waitlist within the same session. They tell another Venezuelan abroad about it within 48 hours.

## Positioning

The only service built specifically for Venezuelan diaspora families who need trusted presence for the people they left behind.

## Conversion & proof

- **Primary CTA**: Join the waitlist ("Únete a la lista de espera") — one-time form with name, WhatsApp or email, and city where family lives.
- **Secondary CTA**: "Habla con nosotros" via WhatsApp — for visitors not ready to commit, who want to ask questions first.
- **The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds**: "Presente, aunque tú no puedas estarlo."
- **Belief ladder** — what a visitor must believe, in order, before signing up:
  1. This problem is real and no one else is solving it ("7.7 millones se fueron; su familia se quedó")
  2. Compas are real people, verified, and trustworthy (active Compa profiles with photos, ratings, visit counts)
  3. The service actually works — a real WhatsApp report bridges the distance (the floating "Última visita" card, family testimonials)
  4. Compaz is launching now and my spot is limited (waitlist framing, "recién empezamos")
- **Proof on hand**: Four active, verified Compas with photos, ratings, and family testimonials. Founder personal origin story (Juan Tenreiro, lived the problem). Real family quotes. Press mentions and external validation (to be linked when available).

## Brand Personality

**Cálido, confiable, cercano** — warm, trustworthy, close-by. The brand feels like a trusted neighbor who happens to run a service, not a company that has decided to enter the care market. Voice is direct and personal, written in Venezuelan Spanish, without corporate softening. It names the problem honestly ("no siempre hay alguien de confianza") and the solution concretely ("un Compa verificado"). Emotional register: reassurance without sentimentality, urgency without pressure.

## Anti-references

- **Medical / clinical** (hospital websites, insurance platforms): cold, formal, institutional. Compaz is human-first; the Compa is a companion before anything else.
- **Generic SaaS startup aesthetic** (purple gradient cards, metric dashboards, "scale your team" energy): Compaz is not a workflow tool. It is a human service for a specific cultural community.
- **Charity / NGO framing** (poverty imagery, pity-driven copy, donation-platform tone): the families are not charity cases; they are capable people navigating an extraordinary situation.
- **Remittance app** (Zelle, Western Union, purely transactional): money is not the relationship; presence is.

## Design Principles

1. **Presence over distance** — every design decision should make the service feel near and personal, not remote and digital. Show real names, real faces, real neighborhoods. The product bridges distance; the design must not recreate it.
2. **Trust through specificity** — vague warmth is not trustworthy. Concrete details are: "34 visitas realizadas," "Chacao, Caracas," "acompañó a mi mamá al cardiólogo." Trust is built by being specific, not by claiming to be trustworthy.
3. **Venezuelan, not pan-Latino** — the cultural specificity is the product. The problem exists because of Venezuela's particular situation. Don't soften it for broader appeal; that specificity is what makes Compaz recognizable to its real audience.
4. **Urgency without manipulation** — the waitlist is real, the launch is real, the scarcity is real. Communicate it honestly. No countdown timers, no "only 3 spots left" tricks. The urgency comes from the problem, not from dark patterns.
5. **Warmth first, technology second** — the WhatsApp report, the verification process, and the scheduling are technology; the Compa's presence is the product. Design should make the human relationship feel primary and the infrastructure feel invisible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Fix obvious contrast failures (two identified: `--text-light` #8B7BAA on light backgrounds at ~3.7:1, and footer copyright text at ~3.2:1). No formal WCAG target, but legibility is non-negotiable for an audience that includes older Venezuelan family members who may be viewing reports or the site with aging eyes. Ensure form inputs are clearly labeled, keyboard navigation works on the waitlist form, and the accordion is screen-reader accessible.
